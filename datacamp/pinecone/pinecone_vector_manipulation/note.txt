here are two main methods for accessing vectors from a Pinecone vector database: fetching and querying. Fetching involves finding and returning a record using its ID.
This is normally done to explore and verify particular records from the index. Querying involves sending a vector to the index, and returning the vector or vectors that are most similar to it.
If vectors were books, fetching would be like using the ISBN, or International Standard Book Number, to find a book. Querying is like searching for books that are similar to one we already own. 

Read units, or RUs, are a Pinecone-specific measure of the resources consumed during reading operations. Fetching, querying, and listing vectors all consume read units. For fetching,
1RU is consumed for every batch of 10 records retrieved.

2. Metadata filtering

Vectors often have associated metadata stored in the index. This metadata can be strings, numbers, Booleans, or true/false values, and lists of strings. This metadata can be used to filter
the vectors that are searched through during the query, reducing the search space and query latency.
